## Context

Current file sizes do not bound complete post-transaction or post-start copies. Hidden companions can escape a basename-prefix scan, and symlink targets can otherwise be omitted or counted twice. A subtotal of justified upper-bound terms may exceed a selected budget while total demand remains unknown; it is not an actual-demand lower bound or demonstrated disk shortage.

## Why

Incomplete accounting can falsely establish fit, shortage or protection of committed new state.

## How to apply

Bind every copy, later DB/companion, startup, metadata and output obligation to the actual implementation and lifetime. Derive companion names from all filename constructions, including leading-dot atomic temporary files. Keep missing totals unknown and identify justified subtotals. Use the exact approved logical budget and selected physical-policy denominator; group shared-pool demands and count free space once. Finite allocation envelopes plus fresh stage checks are a risk-control policy, not a full physical upper bound or guaranteed reserve. Aggregate write-before accounting must cover every writer; per-file writers and post-copy counting do not substitute.

Use the owning formal decision to classify indirect host-managed OS effects. A cache-like name does not turn a direct process/native or protected/task-owned write into an excluded effect. Keep any excluded amount `unknown/excluded_by_policy`, never zero, and keep logical exclusion separate from physical-risk acceptance. Excluded OS allocation can still consume the margin after a modeled space check passes.

Keep private consistent cold copies and isolated restore as actual prerequisites. If a postcommit copy fails, preserve the original new-state DB, sidecars, partial destinations and precopies, stop source application/startup, and use separately authorized reconciled forward recovery. Precopies cannot reconstruct unprotected committed state; do not restore old state, delete assets or reset attempts to make capacity appear adequate.
